**Kafka and Redis Streams take one `startFrom`: `'beginning' | 'end'`, default `'beginning'` on both.** The option had two vocabularies with opposite defaults: `'earliest' | 'latest'` on Kafka (default `earliest`), `'new' | 'beginning'` on Redis Streams (default `new`), while NATS always delivers the whole stream. A consumer deployed after its publisher replayed history on Kafka and NATS and missed it on Redis Streams. A new consumer group now starts at the beginning of what its topics or streams hold on the three, and `startFrom: 'end'` skips it. The former words are refused at construction with `StartFromRefusedError` (`START_FROM_REFUSED`), whose message names the word that replaced them; no alias is kept. On Redis Streams a group the server lost while its stream stayed is created again at the beginning too: what the stream still holds is delivered again, where it was skipped.

To move: on Kafka, write `startFrom: 'beginning'` for `'earliest'` and `startFrom: 'end'` for `'latest'`; a bus that passed neither changes nothing. On Redis Streams, a bus that passed `startFrom: 'new'`, or no `startFrom` and counts on a new group skipping what its streams already hold, passes `startFrom: 'end'`; without it a consumer name that has no group yet reads the whole stream at its first start (the groups that exist keep their position). `startFrom: 'beginning'` on Redis Streams stays as it is and can go.

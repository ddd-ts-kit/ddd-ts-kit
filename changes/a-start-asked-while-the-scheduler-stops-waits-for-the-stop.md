**A `start()` of the scheduler asked while a `stop()` is in progress begins once the stop ended.** `InProcessJobScheduler.stop()` waits for the occurrence a job is handling; a `start()` asked meanwhile followed the job again at once, and the occurrence in progress armed its own timer when it ended. The job then had two timers, every wake-up armed two more, and the next `stop()` cancelled one of them: the other fired later and ran an occurrence after the scheduler had stopped. A start or a stop now runs whole before the next one begins, so a start asked during a stop finds every job asleep, and each job has one timer at a time (#900).
